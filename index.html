Inside Every Drop of Sugar Defender, You'll Find:

1

Eleuthero

Eleuthero, also known as Siberian ginseng, is a traditional botanical used in wellness supplements. It is commonly included in formulations designed to support everyday energy, stamina, and the body's response to normal daily stress.
2

Coleus (Coleus forskohlii)

Coleus is a plant used in some herbal supplements for metabolism and weight-management support. Its active compound, forskolin, has been studied for various biological effects, although evidence for meaningful benefits in humans remains limited.
3

Maca Root

Maca is a plant native to the Andes, traditionally consumed as a food and used in wellness products. It is often included in supplements intended to support vitality, stamina, and general well-being.
4

African Mango

African mango is derived from the fruit and seeds of Irvingia gabonensis. It is commonly used in dietary supplements marketed for metabolism and weight management. Research on its effectiveness is limited, and further high-quality studies are needed.
5

Guarana

Guarana is a tropical plant whose seeds naturally contain caffeine. It is commonly used in products designed to promote alertness and energy. Because it contains caffeine, it may cause sleep disturbances, nervousness, or a faster heartbeat in sensitive individuals.
6

Gymnema Sylvestre

Gymnema is a traditional herb used in Ayurvedic practices. It has attracted research interest for its possible relationship with glucose metabolism and sweet-taste perception. However, its inclusion in a supplement does not establish that the finished product controls blood sugar.
7

Ginseng

Ginseng is a well-known botanical used in traditional wellness practices. It is commonly included in supplements formulated for energy and general vitality. Research into its effects on glucose metabolism is ongoing, and results can vary depending on the preparation and dosage.
8

Chromium

Chromium is an essential trace mineral involved in normal nutrient metabolism. It is frequently included in nutritional supplements intended to support glucose metabolism. The benefits of chromium supplementation vary, and evidence does not establish that it treats diabetes.
